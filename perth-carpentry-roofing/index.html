FAQ · Roofing & carpentry

Good questions, straight answers.

General guidance on roofing and outdoor builds in Perth. Every home is different, so ask Mat about yours.

Roof framing is the timber structure: the rafters, trusses and battens that set the roof's shape and carry its weight. Roofing is the cover that goes on top, such as tiles or metal sheeting. Perth Carpentry and Roofing specialises in the framing and handles roofing work too, so the structure is built with the finished roof in mind.

Common signs are water stains on ceilings, drips after rain, cracked or slipped tiles, rusted or lifting sheets, sagging lines along the ridge, and flaking or rotting timber at the gable ends. If you notice any of these, it's worth getting the roof looked at before the next run of winter rain.

As soon as you find it. Most of Perth's rain falls between May and September, so small leaks that go unnoticed over summer tend to show up, and get worse, once winter arrives. Booking repairs in the drier months also makes the work easier to schedule.

Barge boards are the timber boards running along the sloping edges of a gable roof. They protect the roof edge from weather and are often the first timber to suffer on older homes. Depending on their condition, they can be repaired, partly replaced or fully replaced, and Mat can advise which suits your home after seeing them.

A scribe is the timber board along the edge of a tiled roof at the gable, where the tiles finish. Over time it can weather, split or rot, leaving the edge tiles poorly supported and the roof edge open to the weather. Replacing it restores a solid, neat edge.

Often, yes. In Western Australia many patios, pergolas, carports and decks need a building permit, and the rules vary by local council and by the size and position of the structure. Check with your council early; Mat can talk through the design so you know what to ask.
